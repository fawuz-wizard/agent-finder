## Problem Statement

Customers need to find an Orange Money agent nearby for a specific cash-in or cash-out request. The app can identify open agents by location and use transaction activity as evidence, but it cannot know an agent's exact cash or e-float at arrival time. The flow must help customers choose without hiding the only nearby option or overstating what the activity signal proves.

## What Makes This Cool

The finder can say, “Here is the closest option, here is the option with the strongest evidence for your request, and here is what we do not know.” Customers can inspect the shop before choosing whether to get directions.

## Constraints

- Hackathon prototype scope: one week.
- Cash In and Cash Out only.
- Search within 500 metres first, then widen through the selected area and wider service zone when needed to find open agents.
- Do not expose agent balances or private capacity figures.
- Activity score is a proxy for transaction availability, not a guarantee.
- A low activity score means less evidence; it does not prove the agent cannot serve the customer.
- Shop details must appear before the map. Directions open only after the customer chooses Get directions.
- Keep the result set small and readable. Show no more than one or two fallback agents outside 500 metres in the first version.

## Premises

1. **500 metres is the normal customer search area.** This keeps the first result set relevant and manageable.
2. **Activity evidence can rank likely options but cannot verify current liquidity.** Customer-facing language must preserve that uncertainty.
3. **Every open agent in the core area remains findable.** Weak activity evidence must not silently remove the customer's only nearby option.
4. **A customer should choose when to open directions.** Tapping an agent opens shop details with the map closed.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Fixed 500 metre list

Show open agents within 500 metres, with Recommended ranked by activity evidence and Nearest ordered by distance. If no one looks likely, show the nearby agents with an uncertainty message and no farther alternatives.

- Effort: S
- Risk: Low
- Reuses the current tabs and agent detail page.
- Keeps search simple, but can leave the customer without a useful next option when the local area has no likely match.

### Approach B: 500 metre core with an area-wide fallback (recommended)

Keep all open agents within 500 metres available. If that core area has no likely activity match, widen the search to the 20 kilometre Freetown service radius. Show up to two nearest open options beyond 500 metres in a separate “Further away” group. A sole low-evidence agent within 500 metres remains visible and is labeled as the only nearby option, not as Recommended. If there is no open agent in the 500 metre core, widen the search to find the nearest open agents within the service radius.

- Effort: M
- Risk: Low to medium
- Reuses the existing distance calculation, result cards, tabs, and detail route.
- Gives the customer a next step while keeping the 500 metre list as the main experience.
- Uses a city-scale outer limit for the prototype and returns at most two farther results.

### Approach C: Always append farther alternatives

Whenever the search has fewer than a chosen number of results, append one or two open agents beyond 500 metres, even if an in-radius likely match exists.

- Effort: M
- Risk: Medium
- May reveal a stronger option outside the core area.
- Adds more choices to every search and needs a clear definition of what makes a farther option useful.

## Recommended Approach

Use Approach B for the hackathon. Treat 500 metres as the preferred core area, not a hard stop. Rank agents by transaction-specific activity evidence for Recommended, and sort all open core-area agents by distance for Nearest. A recommended agent can also appear in Nearest.

Do not describe a low score as “cannot handle.” Use a customer phrase such as “Availability unclear for this request” when evidence is weak or missing. Keep the approved “Can likely handle your request” phrase for a positive activity signal, while making clear that it is not a guarantee.

If no likely match exists inside 500 metres, retain all open core agents and add at most two open candidates from farther in the 20 kilometre Freetown service radius under a separate “Further away” heading. This keeps at least one open option visible whenever one exists in the service radius, without filling the screen with hundreds of results. If likely core matches do exist, defer farther alternatives unless they provide a clear, explainable improvement.

If the only open agent inside 500 metres has weak evidence, show it prominently as “Only open agent within 500 m” with the uncertainty phrase. Do not put it in Recommended. The customer may still inspect the shop and choose directions.

## Customer Flow

1. Customer chooses Cash In or Cash Out, enters an amount, and selects an area or simulated location.
2. Search finds open agents within the 500 metre core area.
3. Recommended shows agents with the strongest relevant activity evidence and the status “Can likely handle your request.” This is a likelihood statement, not a promise.
4. Nearest shows the open core-area agents in distance order, including agents with unclear or weak evidence. Recommended agents may also appear here.
5. If no likely core match exists, show a short no-match explanation, preserve the in-radius options, and add up to two farther options in a separate group.
6. Tapping any agent opens its shop details first. Show name, location, open status, services, hours, distance, request-specific availability wording, and freshness.
7. The customer chooses Get directions to reveal the map, Call if the agent opted in, or goes back to results. Looking at the shop or map alone does not count as a visit.

## Results Screen States

The screen should respond to the search outcome instead of always opening an empty Recommended view.

### One or more likely matches inside 500 metres

- Show the `Recommended` and `Nearest` tabs with counts, for example `Recommended (2)` and `Nearest (5)`.
- Open on Recommended. It contains only likely matches, ordered by activity evidence and then distance.
- Nearest contains all open agents in the 500 metre core, sorted by distance. Recommended agents can appear in both tabs.
- If a closer agent has uncertain availability, add a short link such as “2 closer options have uncertain availability · See Nearest.” This keeps those options discoverable without labeling them as recommendations.
- Do not add farther agents in the first version when there is already a likely core match.

### No likely matches, but one or more open agents inside 500 metres

- Do not leave the customer on an empty Recommended tab.
- Replace the tabs with a clear message: “No likely match in 500 m. These nearby agents are open, but availability for your request is uncertain.”
- Show the core agents directly, nearest first. If there is exactly one, label it “Only open agent within 500 m.” Do not call it Recommended.
- Add up to two farther open options under “Further away,” if found.

### No open agents inside 500 metres

- Say “No open agents within 500 m.”
- Widen the search from 500 metres to the 20 kilometre Freetown service radius. Show up to two nearest open candidates under “Further away,” including their actual distance.
- Only show an empty result if there is no open agent in that service radius. Then say so clearly and offer the customer a way to change the area or search again. Never invent an agent or show an offline agent as open.

## Success Criteria

- A customer can tell why an agent appears under Recommended versus Nearest.
- The customer is never shown an empty Recommended tab as the only result state.
- When recommendations exist, closer uncertain agents are discoverable without appearing as recommendations.
- A low activity score is presented as uncertainty, not proof the agent cannot serve them.
- A sole open agent inside 500 metres remains visible even when there is no likely match.
- When no likely core match exists, no more than two farther candidates are shown in a separate group.
- When the 500 metre core has no open agent, the search widens through the selected area and service zone to surface the nearest open candidate when one exists.
- Tapping a result opens shop details with the map closed; the map opens only after Get directions.
- The customer can still choose a closer uncertain option after seeing its status.

## Open Questions

- Confirm the maximum service radius with Orange before treating 20 kilometres as a live-product rule.
- Define the activity-score threshold and freshness rules for “Can likely handle your request” with the team; do not imply that this threshold verifies a live balance.
- Revisit whether farther options are useful when a likely match already exists inside 500 metres, after trying the no-match fallback.

## Next Steps

1. Agree on the wording for weak or missing activity evidence.
2. Confirm the selected-area boundary used for fallback search and the one-or-two result cap.
3. Build one demo scenario with a likely core match, one with only an uncertain core agent, and one with no open core agents but farther options.
4. Keep result-card taps on shop details; make map display an explicit customer action.

## The Assignment

Walk an Orange stakeholder through the three scenarios above without explaining the labels first. Ask them what they think the activity status means and whether the farther options feel useful. Record any misunderstanding before choosing final customer wording.
